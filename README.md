# Lis App

Connects an Android phone to a lis desktop over the local network, a USB cable, or a relay server: phone stats and
battery alerts on the PC, files both ways, locking and unlocking the PC from the phone, finding the
phone, the shell's todos, notes, goals and calendar on the phone, the PC's media on the phone's lock screen,
action buttons, and the phone's camera as an OBS source, with photos from it straight to the PC. It
replaces KDE Connect and DroidCam for these.

## Layout

```
core/      Rust, shared: protocol, pairing, TLS, sessions, file transfer
daemon/    Rust, PC: lis-daemon, listens for the phone and serves the shell over a Unix socket
ffi/       Rust: the core as a library for Android, with Kotlin bindings generated by UniFFI
video/     Rust, shared: the camera stream's packets
relay/     Rust: lis-relay, the server that joins the two when they can't reach each other
obs-plugin/  Rust: the "Lis Camera" source for OBS
android/   Kotlin and Compose: the system hooks and the screens
```

The shell side lives in the lis config: `phone/`, `settings/pages/PhonePage.qml`, and the services
that keep todos, notes, goals and calendar notes.

## Security

- All traffic is TLS 1.3, both ways, with each device's own self-signed certificate.
- Pairing is numeric comparison: both devices show a 6-digit code, and a person accepts on both.
  The phone commits to its nonce before seeing the PC's, so an attacker can't search for
  certificates that produce a matching code.
- The PC only accepts pairing for 2 minutes after you press Pair, and one phone at a time.
- After pairing, each side pins the other's certificate fingerprint. Until both sides trust each
  other, only hello and pairing messages move: incoming data closes the connection, and outgoing
  data is dropped.
- Keys on the PC are readable only by your user.
- File names from the other device are reduced to a plain name, and nothing is overwritten.
- Action buttons send only an id. Their commands stay on the PC and never reach the phone, so a paired
  phone can't run anything else. They do run while the PC is locked, so don't add one you wouldn't
  want a stolen phone to press.
- Unlocking takes a signature, over a one-time challenge from the PC, from a key in the phone's secure
  hardware that signs only right after a strong biometric check, and stops working if a fingerprint
  is added. So neither the app's files nor the pairing key can unlock the PC. The key has to be
  approved on the PC, which only someone at the unlocked PC can do. It works from the first lock after
  a boot, so on a PC that logs in by itself, the phone is enough to get in. The keyring still takes the
  password. After 3 wrong signatures, the next unlock takes the password too. The daemon's socket has
  no command that unlocks, though a program already running as your user could still impersonate the
  daemon to the shell.

## Build

```sh
./install.sh
```

This installs `lis-daemon` to `~/.local/bin`, restarting it if it's running, installs the OBS plugin,
builds the app into `output/lis-app.apk`, and the relay server into `output/lis-relay` with its
`lis-relay.service`. It doesn't touch the phone or a server. Use `--daemon-only`, `--plugin-only`,
`--apk-only` or `--relay-only` for less. The plugin also needs OBS's and FFmpeg's headers, and
rebuilding after a major update of either.

It needs Rust, a JDK (17 or newer), and the Android SDK in `~/Android/Sdk` (or `ANDROID_HOME`) with
its licenses accepted and NDK 30.0.16248370. It passes `https_proxy` on to Gradle, as Java ignores
it.

## Use

The PC listens on TCP 47420 and advertises itself over mDNS (UDP 5353). With ufw:

```sh
sudo ufw allow from 192.168.0.0/16 to any port 47420 proto tcp
sudo ufw allow from 192.168.0.0/16 to any port 5353 proto udp
```

1. In the shell: Settings, Phone Sync, turn it on, press Pair.
2. In the app: choose the PC, or enter its address.
3. Check that both show the same code, and accept on both.
4. In the app's Settings (the gear on its home screen), allow notifications, background use and, on
   HyperOS, Autostart. The gear has a red dot while something it needs isn't allowed.

## Settings

The gear on the app's home screen. Besides the permissions above, it sets the theme (the system's, dark
or light), what the home screen shows, whether the connection lasts when you leave the app and starts
with the phone, how often the phone's figures go to the PC, which notifications show, the PC's media on
the lock screen and its volume step, how long and how loud Find my phone rings, the camera's defaults,
and the first day of the calendar's week. It also shows the paired PC, and unpairs it.

## Find my phone

**Find Phone** in the shell's Phone panel rings it as an alarm, at full volume and with vibration,
which sounds even on silent. It stops from the phone or the panel, or by itself after a minute. The
app's Settings can change how long, and turn off the full volume or the vibration. Total silence Do
Not Disturb can still mute alarms.

The PC notifies you once when the phone's battery falls to 20% and to 10%, and when it's charged to
100%.

## Todos, notes, goals and calendar

These are the shell's own files, `todo.json`, `notes.json`, `goals.json` and `calendar.json` in
`~/.config/lis`, so the
phone and the shell show the same things. The phone keeps a copy and works without the PC. Changes go
out within a second or two of a save, and catch up on reconnecting.

The shell's data has no edit times, so both sides are compared to the last copy they agreed on, item
by item. Changes to different items or fields all survive. If both changed the same field, the PC's
copy wins, except that a note rewritten on both keeps its older version as a copy named "(conflict)".
An item deleted on one side and edited on the other stays. A save in the shell that lands within a
few microseconds of one from the phone can still lose to it, as the shell's saves take no locks.

Before each change from a phone, the daemon keeps the old file in
`~/.local/share/lis-app/sync-backups`, the last 10 of each. A file over about 900 KB doesn't sync, and
the app says so. Unpairing removes the phone's copies and leaves the PC's.

Calendar notes can have a time, and repeat weekly, monthly or yearly. Reminders and the morning summary come
from the PC only.

Reordering and restoring from the trash are only in the shell. A private note is covered on the phone
as in the shell, which isn't encryption.

## Media

What's playing on the PC shows on the phone's lock screen and on the app's home screen, with play and
pause, next, previous and volume. It uses `playerctl` and `wpctl`, and `curl` for cover art from the
web. Any player that supports MPRIS works. While it shows, the phone's volume buttons turn the PC's
volume, as with a Chromecast.

## Action buttons

Add them in the shell: Settings, Phone Sync, Phone Actions. Each is a name and a command, run with
`sh` as your user. They're also in `~/.config/lis/phone-actions.json`, to edit by hand.

`lis-daemon obs` controls OBS, for buttons like these. It reads the address and password from OBS's
settings, once its server is on: in OBS, Tools, WebSocket Server Settings.

```sh
~/.local/bin/lis-daemon obs scene "Main"
~/.local/bin/lis-daemon obs record
~/.local/bin/lis-daemon obs stream
~/.local/bin/lis-daemon obs scenes   # lists them
```

## USB

A cable is steadier than Wi-Fi for the camera, and charges the phone. It needs `adb`, and USB debugging
on the phone.

1. In the shell: Settings, Phone Sync, turn on **Use the USB cable**. This keeps `adb reverse` set up.
2. In the app: turn on **Use the USB cable**.

The phone reaches the PC through the tunnel, and the same certificates are checked as over Wi-Fi. With
it on, the app doesn't go looking on the network, so turn it off to go back to Wi-Fi.

## Relay server

For when the phone can't reach the PC directly: on mobile data, or on someone else's Wi-Fi. Both
connect out to `lis-relay` on a server they can reach, which joins them. The relay only passes bytes:
the same TLS runs inside, end to end, with the same certificate checks, so it can't read or change
anything. It sees the PC's fingerprint, which it routes by, and how much goes through. Each request
carries an HMAC of the relay's password over a fresh nonce, so the password never crosses the network.

- Only phones already paired on the network or the cable get through. Pairing never goes over it.
- Files, the camera and Snap to PC are off through it, to save data. Everything else works, fingerprint
  unlock included.
- The phone tries the PC's address first, then the relay. Off Wi-Fi it goes straight to the relay, and
  Wi-Fi being off no longer pauses it. It keeps looking for the PC nearby, and leaves the relay once it
  finds it. Never on the USB cable.

To run it on a server, build it here, which puts it in `output/` with its `config.toml` and
`lis-relay.service`:

```sh
./install.sh --relay-only
scp output/lis-relay output/config.toml output/lis-relay.service you@server:
# on the server
sudo install -m755 lis-relay /usr/local/bin/
sudo install -Dm600 config.toml /etc/lis-relay/config.toml
sudo nano /etc/lis-relay/config.toml       # set auth.password: head -c 24 /dev/urandom | base64
sudo lis-relay --check --config /etc/lis-relay/config.toml
sudo install -Dm644 lis-relay.service /etc/systemd/system/lis-relay.service
sudo systemctl enable --now lis-relay
sudo ufw allow 47421/tcp
journalctl -u lis-relay -f                 # what it's doing
```

The binary is static (musl, built with clang when there's no musl-gcc), so it runs on any x86-64 Linux
server. Without either, it falls back to this PC's glibc, which an older server may not have.

### config.toml

Only `auth.password` is needed; every other key has a default, and the file explains each.
`lis-relay --print-config` prints it again. A misspelled key or a bad value stops it from starting,
with the reason, and `--check` tests a file without starting. After a change, `sudo systemctl restart
lis-relay`: the devices reconnect by themselves. The config stays readable only by root, and systemd
hands the service a copy.

| Section | What it sets |
| --- | --- |
| `[server]` | `address` and `port` to listen on (47421), and `log`: `quiet`, `info` or `debug` |
| `[auth]` | `password` or `password_file`; `failure_delay_ms` before answering a wrong one; banning an address after `max_failures` within `failure_window_minutes`, for `ban_minutes` |
| `[limits]` | `max_connections`, `max_connections_per_ip`, `max_pcs` waiting, `max_tunnels` in all and `max_tunnels_per_pc`; `tunnel_kbps` each way per tunnel (512), `total_kbps` for all of them, `daily_mb_per_pc` (500, both ways, UTC days, counted since start) and `max_tunnel_hours` |
| `[timeouts]` | `request_secs`, `answer_secs` for the PC to answer, `idle_secs` of silence that ends a tunnel (30 or more), `listener_ping_secs` (under 75) |
| `[access]` | `allow_pcs` by fingerprint (the shell copies this PC's from Relay Server), `allow_ips` and `deny_ips` as addresses or ranges like `203.0.113.0/24` |

Without a config file, `LIS_RELAY_PASSWORD` (or `LIS_RELAY_PASSWORD_FILE`), `LIS_RELAY_PORT` and
`LIS_RELAY_KBPS` are enough for a quick run, with the rest at their defaults.

Then in the shell: Settings, Phone Sync, Relay Server: the address, port and password. The PC sends them
to the phone the next time they're connected, so the phone needs no setup, and the app's Settings show
which relay it knows.

## Phone unlock

1. In the app's Settings, under Fingerprint Unlock: **Unlock the PC with your fingerprint**.
2. In the shell: Settings, Phone Sync, Phone Unlock, **Allow**.

Then the fingerprint prompt pops up on the phone when you lock the PC by hand, and when you press a key
at its lock screen, as that first prompt will be long gone when you come back. It doesn't when the PC
locks itself after being idle, or when you lock it from the phone, and it stays away for 20 seconds
after you dismiss it. While the camera is running it won't cover the picture.

For it to pop up, allow what the app's Settings ask for there: Android's full-screen and draw-over
permissions, and on HyperOS its own pop-up and lock screen ones. Without them the phone shows a banner to
tap. The "PC is locked" notification and the **Unlock PC** button in the app also bring up the prompt.

The PC notifies you each time it's unlocked this way. The keyring stays as it was, so after a boot it stays
locked until you type the password, at the lock screen or when an app asks for it.

## Camera

Video only, H.264 from the phone's hardware encoder, at 60 fps or 30. It's 1080p unless you choose
another 16:9 size, from 360p up to 4K, out of those the camera has.

1. In the app, open Camera and press Start. It only runs while that screen is showing, and stops as
   soon as you leave the app or lock the phone.
2. In OBS, add the **Lis Camera** source.

The picture follows the way the phone is held. **Lock landscape** or **Lock portrait** keeps its
shape in OBS whatever the phone does. A locked picture still turns over when the phone does, so it
stays the right way up.

The app says when the camera doesn't offer the frame rate you chose. `adb logcat -s LisCamera` shows
what it does offer.

It uses its own encrypted connection on the same port, and only a paired phone can send to it. When
the connection can't keep up, the bitrate drops until it can, climbing back once it's been clear for a
while. If frames still pile up, they're dropped until the next keyframe rather than the delay growing.

## Snap to PC

**Snap to PC** on the Camera screen takes a photo while the camera runs, with no break in the stream.
**Take Photo** in the shell's Phone panel asks for one from the PC. It lands in `~/Pictures/Phone`, and
on the PC's clipboard as a PNG, ready to paste. A notification shows it: click it to open the photo, or
press **Show in Folder**. In the shell, Settings, Phone Sync, Snap to PC picks another folder and turns
the copying off.

The photo is upright the way the phone is held, whatever the stream's lock. It's the largest size the
camera allows next to the stream, the full sensor on most phones; `adb logcat -s LisCamera` shows which.
A camera that can't take photos while streaming still streams, and the button stays off. Copying needs
`wl-copy`, from wl-clipboard.

## Test

```sh
cargo test
```

The tests run real TLS on localhost and the real daemon binary, with the tools it runs, like
`hyprctl`, `notify-send` and `playerctl`, stubbed. `cargo run -p lis-daemon --example fake_phone` stands in for the phone while working on
the PC side. It can loop a video file as the camera, and answer Take Photo with a JPEG; its header has
the command that makes the video.

`cargo test -p lis-camera` tests the plugin by loading it through libobs, with no OBS window or
settings. `cargo run -p lis-camera --example watch` decodes the stream without OBS and reports the
frame rate and delay.
